# Adjusted-spawn board fall: first eight-frame contact audit (2026-09-23)

Read-only audit of `artifacts/controlled-skating-release30-adjusted-pose-trace-2026-09-23.log` and the current Rust source. **High** confidence for the logged Rust sequence and called Rust stages; **exact** for the cited native operation order already recorded in `native-initial-wheel-contact-source-2026-09-23.md` and `assembly-ground-run-integration.md`; **hypothesis** for any cause of a native-versus-Rust pose difference. There is no matched native eight-frame contact/pose trace. This Rust log does not include the solver's `counts` or ground-job `report`, so it does not show whether frame 1 produced zero, some, or many terrain Contact rows.

## What the first eight frames actually show

The initial Part6 matrix is upright with translation `[588.8617,-54.281002,996.5048]`. Frame 1 starts Ground, Processed wheels `0`, wheel height/distances `0` (constructor output). After its solve Part6 is at Y `-54.44767`, about `0.166668` lower, with a small tilt. At the next `BeforeActor`, all four wheel distances and height are `0.093501695`, Body no-wheel time is `0.016666668`, and Processed wheels remain `0`. The nonzero distances prove four wheel-line results were consumed; they do **not** prove four solved contacts or Body `inContact` bits. Native `CollisionInfo::Reset` writes zero height/distances, `EndWheelLineTest` writes the `0.2` height fallback then updates it for individual hits, and Body PostPhysics separately counts solved contacting wheels (`0x82b23630`, `0x82b24700`, `0x82b372ec`).

At frame 2 `AfterSolver`, Part6 up-axis Y is `0.9408485` and Y is `-54.5265`; at frame 3 `BeforeActor`, the four distances are zero and height is `0.2`. Thereafter each observed pre-Actor height through frame 8 is `0.2`, with Processed wheels `0`. The `0.2` value is the wheel-line fallback in `SkateboardWheelLines::try_end`; paired zero distances indicate no published hit for each wheel in that batch. They do not reveal whether the separate Assembly/ground contact job made contacts. Part6 up-axis Y declines to `0.80847657`, `0.63092583`, `0.44956717`, and `0.30067766` after frames 3 through 6, then `0.15078872` after frame 7 and `0.0071662064` after frame 8. The substantial roll is visible by frame 2, four frames before the state transition.

Frame 6 is still Ground at `BeforePreSolve` and is `WipeoutGround` at `BeforeSolver`, with the *same* Part6 matrix at both observations. Thus the selected physical state changes during pre-solve, before that frame's solver. The log does not identify a wipeout request type or which earlier Ground post-physics detector set it; assigning the request to deck flip, force, or displacement from these matrix snapshots would be conjecture. Frame 6's later root jump is downstream of this transition and cannot explain the earlier board roll.

## Connected Rust path and first unresolved boundary

`SelectedPlayerTick::try_step_queued` observes `BeforePreSolve`, calls `try_prepare_ground_pre_solve`, observes `BeforeSolver`, calls `try_step_authored_ground`, observes `AfterSolver`, then calls `try_post_solve_selected_ground` (`selected_player_tick.rs:455-486`). The Ground frame starts the dynamic body phase, refreshes SkaterPool publications, normalizes drives, runs the CPU Assembly/ground job over the retained static pool, imports its records to the Contact manager, builds Joint/Drive rows, runs shared Horus and integrates bodies, exports contact spies/feedback, and creates physical contact lists (`skateboard_ground_frame.rs:104-240`). The CPU job traverses publication bounds, resident meshes and capped KD leaf ranges, rips triangles, and passes them to `SimulationGroundAssemblyRun::try_run` (`simulation_assembly_ground_job.rs:193-315`). Run gates each volume on frozen/Aggregate state, culling, static body state, enabled/sole-volume rule, triangle overlap and narrow-phase contact points before writing a record (`simulation_ground_contact.rs:317-455`). After solve, board post-physics first ends the retained wheel-line batch, then consumes physical contacts for Body output (`physical_player_board_post_outer_live.rs:123`; `physical_player_board_postphysics.rs:143-185`). Consequently Processed wheel count, wheel-line distance, terrain Contact count, and Part6 motion are four different observations.

The **earliest known source-schedule omission** on this live path is the flags3 counterpart/assembly-pair collision work: `skateboard_ground_frame.rs` explicitly calls its flags3 `Collision2` a no-op and says the isolated synchronous ground job lacks the counterpart/pair pipeline (lines 99-102, 145-150). The first opportunity for that omission to matter is before the frame-1 ground job/solve, earlier than the frame-2 roll and frame-6 wipeout. This is a real implementation gap, **not evidence that missing pair contacts caused this fall**. The current log also cannot distinguish a terrain job with zero rows from one with rows on other bodies, ineffective normals/impulses, or contacts that fail Body's wheel classification. The original-data component probe's C/J/D `6/6/3` at a controlled Rob's Park placement proves the ground producer can make and solve contacts; it does not establish the counts at this adjusted spawn under this Actor force path (`authored-skater-ground-publication-path.md`).

The first observation that needs explanation is therefore frame 1's solved Part6 displacement and contact publication, **not** the frame-6 state change. The displacement's approximate `1/60 * 10` magnitude is an observation only: the log lacks pre-solve Part6 velocity, force, Contact rows and solver impulses, so attributing it to a specific velocity/force is unsupported. No native first-frame values are available to label even that displacement a verified native divergence. The adjusted-spawn position itself has a conditional native source route: command-3 `SkaterManager::OnCommand` calls `AdjustSpawnPosition` before Actor construction, and the cvar/World line determine its output (`0x82600998`, `0x825f1a20`; `controlled-first-skating-spawn-pose-audit-2026-09-23.md`). The Rust initial Y here matches the predicted adjusted result, but the native gate and hit for this exact session still lack a runtime capture.
