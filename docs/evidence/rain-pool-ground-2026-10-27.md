# Authored pool ground for rainwater

The rain height field uses world presentation meshes. Some authored pool floors are absent from those meshes even when the game can render and collide with them. The local `apps/skate2-pc/src/pool-ground.bin` contains seven 73 x 73 half-metre grids sampled from the game's resident static collision geometry around the native pool drain or teleport locator. The grids are supplemental visual support, not new physics geometry. Samples are four-byte little-endian floats, with NaN for a missing collision surface, preceded by two float world origins per grid.

The grids correspond to G.E.D. High, Think Pink, Community, Do-Drop Inn, Poop Deck, Old, and Party Pool, in that order. They are bounded around their actual locations; Party Pool has a tighter bound because another level overlaps it. The runtime accepts a grid only when enough live presentation samples closely match its heights. Existing wet cells retain their previous values. This makes the correction map-specific and a changed/custom map can safely decline it; it is not a universal reconstruction of every map or a physical water simulation.

Evidence was collected with isolated, silent, short game runs and per-run save roots. Each location was compared against its live rain field and resident collision geometry; normal player settings were not changed. Screenshots and quantitative comparisons are in the local review directory. Visual in-game review remains necessary to catch floating surfaces and shore artifacts at different viewpoints.

The authored pools also use restrained, preset-specific shallow water levels above the verified basin floor; previously wet cells remain untouched. This avoids leaving a visually dry pool when sparse geometry breaks the runoff calculation. Levels are visual approximations, not a fluid simulation.

Thunder recording: [Rain and thunder.ogg by Caesar](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_and_thunder.ogg), recorded 2006-08-27 and released into the public domain by its creator. The 2-10 second section is converted to mono 24 kHz PCM with short fades; it is mixed equally into both channels, controlled by the existing rain and SFX sliders, and delayed after the corresponding lightning event using a deterministic estimated distance of 350–1800 m and a sound speed of 343 m/s (roughly 1.0–5.2 seconds). The estimated distance is an artistic model rather than real world coordinates. No operating-system volume or audio device settings are modified.
