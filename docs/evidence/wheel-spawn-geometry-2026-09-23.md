Read-only audit of the recovered native constructor/placement/relocation path, original extracted `skater.vlt/bin` words, current Rust owners, and the adjusted-spawn two-frame release trace. **Exact** applies to the cited native calls, source geometry inputs, and local placement equations; **high** applies to the successful Rust ownership path and trace interpretation. ## Native geometry and spawn chronology

`SkaterManager::OnCommand` command 3 calls `AdjustSpawnPosition` at `0x82600998` before `SpawnPlayer` at `0x826009b4`. The former preserves the axes and, when its network/cvar gate and World line hit permit, replaces translation with the hit plus Y `0.15000001` (`0x825f1a20..0x825f1bb8`). The latter constructs the Actor with that matrix (`0x82600630`). In the physical board constructor, `SkateboardBody::Init` calls `CreateWheels` (`0x82b344f0`), then `InitializeTransforms` (`0x82b2bc10`) after definition/Assembly initialization. This writes detached Parts in order 6,0,1,2,3,4,5. Following island admission, drives, and mandatory hook creation, the outer constructor calls `SkateboardBody::SetTransform(input)` at `0x82b388b4`. The latter reads live deck Part 6, computes one displacement, sets deck 6 and then Parts 0..5 in order, with a fresh live getter before each write (`0x82b34a90..0x82b34d98`). `Part::SetTransform` updates each attached body, so the initial local coordinates are relocated into live body/volume coordinates. The native ordering and source arithmetic are detailed in [initial placement](skateboard-initial-placement.md) and [spawn transform](skateboard-spawn-transform-source.md).

The original extracted Vault has `WheelCapsuleLength=0x00000000`, so `CreateWheels` chooses **Sphere** under its `length > 0` branch; all four Parts share the same fresh sphere volume. `WheelRadius=0x3cfdf3b6`, or about `0.03099999949` world units. The volume has local identity axes and zero translation, flags 1, surface 0, group 5; its collision radius is the stored Vault value. The mass routine's minimum-radius selection and each debug-sphere radius are separate uses of the attribute and do not shift the collision center. These facts are in [wheel definitions](skateboard-wheels.md) and `skateboard_wheels.rs::try_create`.

The original placement words are deck length `0x3f170a3d` (about `0.59`), front/rear offsets each `0xbd54fdf4` (about `-0.052`), truck vertical `0xbd676c8b` (about `-0.0565`), and wheel lateral `0x3dc28f5c` (about `0.095`). ## What the Rust boot actually owns

The successful Rust construction uses `SkateboardWheelAttributes::try_snapshot` and `SkateboardWheelDefinitions::try_create` for the original attribute bindings and sphere volume (`skateboard_wheels.rs`). `SkateboardBodyConstruction::try_construct` builds the seven-part authored session, calls `try_initialize_skateboard_transforms_with_writer` on its detached Parts, and later `PhysicalPlayerAssembly::try_construct_board` passes `inputs.spawn` to `try_set_registered_board_transform` after board/hook admission (`skateboard_body_construction.rs`, `skateboard_initial_transforms.rs`, `physical_player_assembly.rs`). The current controlled facade performs the same-world source-gated terrain line before constructing that player and passes its adjusted matrix (`selected_session_public_facade.rs`). `SkateboardCollisionPublication::read_live_parts` derives live Part transforms from attached bodies and the retained inverse-body metadata; its records pair each Part 0..3 with that shared sphere, `padding=0.05`, and its live transform. Ground Run turns each record into a `GpPrimitive` using that parent transform, with the sphere radius as GP fatness (`skateboard_collision_publication.rs`, `simulation_ground_contact.rs`, `narrow_phase.rs`). This is a connected source-shaped center/radius path, subject to the documented host numeric and immutable-snapshot limits; there is no wheel-radius or offset substituted at publication.

The release trace's first `BeforeSolver` Part 6 translation is `[588.8617,-54.281002,996.5048]`, with up-axis Y exactly 1 and zero deck velocity (`artifacts/controlled-skating-release2-contact-counts-2026-09-23.log`). Its horizontal axes rotate local X/Z, so wheel X/Z world coordinates must be computed from the **full deck matrix**; adding the local X/Z offsets directly to deck X/Z would be wrong. Under this upright orientation and unchanged local Y, the projected center Y is about `-54.337502`, and the sphere bottom about `-54.368502`. The spawn World line hit near deck X/Z was Y about `-54.431`; if that height applied at each wheel X/Z, center-to-ground would be about `0.093498` and sphere-bottom clearance about `0.062498`. The four frame-1 wheel-line results actually published after solve are each `0.093501695`, consistent with that center-line projection at their queried locations. These line results were initiated before the solve and are **not** four wheel sphere contacts. The trace compiled **0 Contact rows** on frame 1 (2 submitted triangles), then **13 rows** on frame 2 (4 triangles), with positive exported spies on deck 6 and two non-wheel bodies only. The filtered spies do not classify all 13 frame-2 rows.

The approximate `0.0625` sphere clearance alone does **not** establish why frame 1 has zero Contacts. The ground Run also uses volume padding `0.05` and generator padding `0.5`, velocity/normal selection, publication bounds, triangle overlap, and narrow-phase/fixup gates (`simulation_assembly_ground_run.rs`, `simulation_ground_contact.rs`). At zero wheel velocity the padding calculation's nominal sum is `0.55`, but its interpretation in each narrow-phase gate cannot be replaced by a simple `radius + padding` threshold. Nor is there a matched native spawn/Contact trace proving that native tick 1 has wheel support. Thus a geometry mismatch or missing native contact is still **unproven**.
